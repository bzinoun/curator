Most app discovery pipelines infer backwards: *"users of this app want a watch version → there's a niche."* This produces false positives at scale.

Counter-example: 197 documented requests for a watch app across 100 apps, median 1 request per app. Each suggestion is one person's comment. When you rank opportunities by these, you're optimizing for attached user bases, not unmet needs.

The forward inference: *"this search term attracts users, the current app shelf is weak or absent → builders should fill this function."* This signal lives in keyword popularity and competitive saturation, not in app review sentiment.

Reality: 197 opinions on 100 unrelated apps is noise. 1 strong keyword signal across a weak shelf is actionable. The lesson: **invert your inference direction.** Measure demand at the market level, not the product level — they answer different questions.
