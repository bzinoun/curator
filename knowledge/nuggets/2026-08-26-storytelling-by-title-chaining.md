A document's top-level headings form a cohesive story if each title's ending phrase becomes the next title's opening. Read the sequence of titles alone—skip the body—and you should grasp the arc.

Example structure: "**the problem:** …is dispersed" → "**dispersed** — tomorrow it fits on one screen" → "**one screen** that poses 11 questions" → "questions, 18 blocks: **the product to scope**."

This creates cognitive continuity and makes navigation **forward-leaning**: readers know where they're going. Works on decks, specs, design documents, and multi-level navigation. The phrase-matching is invisible to casual readers but saves wandering; tested here on an 11-slide kickoff, where it survived revisions without breaking.
