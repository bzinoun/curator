Visual design patterns like decorative side borders, colored bars above headings, and gradient text are emerging as involuntary "tells" that characterize AI-generated interface design. Audit frameworks (e.g., impeccable with 60+ detectors) now flag these patterns as signals of auto-generation.

Why it matters: as generative design tools proliferate, distinguishing involuntary stylistic habits from intentional choices becomes critical. A `.scenario` side-tab accent border or `.card::before` decorative bar might serve accessibility goals (differentiating grouped content), but simultaneously *signals* "this was AI-designed." The insight is **intent + awareness**: a designer can now audit their work against these tells and either eliminate them intentionally or justify their presence. Conversely, deliberately using a tells pattern (e.g., gradient text in a PNG marketing render) is no longer accidental—it's a conscious aesthetic choice, fully defensible if the designer knows what they're invoking.
