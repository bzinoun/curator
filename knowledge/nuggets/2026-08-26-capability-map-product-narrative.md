**Use a capability journey map instead of backlog bullets to communicate product scope.**

Traditional backlogs are lists—stakeholders don't retain them. A stronger narrative: arrange capabilities in a 2D grid where columns mirror the user journey (e.g., *Identify → Understand → Deepen → Act*), rows are feature blocks. Color by status: *built* (dark), *v1* (medium), *future* (light gray).

This achieves three things: readable workflow narrative (left-to-right mirrors actual use), instant scope clarity (visual vs. euphemism), and completeness check (gaps become obvious—is the rightmost column all gray?).

For stakeholder approval and steering committees, this communicates what a spreadsheet cannot—that the product is coherent and deliberately scoped, not incomplete.
