**Slide titles must be conclusions, not subject labels.**

A widespread deck weakness: titles are *subjects* ("The Architecture", "Budget", "Timeline"). Skimming titles teaches only what was *discussed*, not what was *decided*. McKinsey/BCG decks reverse this—each title is a complete *assertion*.

Example shifts:
- "Team" → "A dedicated squad with one subject-matter expert per workstream"
- "Schedule" → "Five iterations lock the October production date"
- "Risk" → "Remaining risk is no longer technical"

This forces two things: clarity (if your title and content don't match, it's visible) and retention (executives grasp the story from titles alone). For steering-committee decks, it's not style, it's how you avoid death by PowerPoint.
